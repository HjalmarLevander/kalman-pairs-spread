Why it fails, and what to fix

  1. The chain of cause is traceable, not mysterious. Phase 1's likelihood-optimal noise parameters push the filter into an over-adaptive regime → the residual spread has a half-life under one day at every percentile tested → a signal that reverts in under a day, traded on daily bars, cannot generate enough magnitude between entries and exits to outrun a realistic ~5bps-per-leg cost against real notional.
  2. Denoising alone can't rescue it. Raising the FFT percentile threshold shrinks trade count and losses (V/MA: −15.5 max DD at p=0 → −3.1 at p=95) but never flips Sharpe positive — it's suppressing the same too-fast signal, not fixing its root cause.
  3. The fix is upstream, in Phase 1 — tested below, not just proposed. Re-fit the Kalman noise parameters with a selection criterion that penalizes short implied half-life, not raw log-likelihood alone.

The fix, tested: constrain the fit away from the degenerate regime

Added a half-life floor to the Phase 1 noise-parameter search: among (delta, obs_cov) grid points, maximize log-likelihood only among those whose resulting spread has OU half-life ≥ 10 days, instead of maximizing log-likelihood alone.

⚠
What this does and doesn't prove. The noise-parameter choice (the half-life floor) was genuinely validated against a holdout the fit never saw. The (p, z_entry, z_exit) trading-rule grid was not independently re-validated out-of-sample — it was chosen by best in-sample Sharpe, so one layer of selection risk remains there. The OOS Kalman filter was also cold-started at 2022-01-01 rather than carrying forward filter state from the fit window, which a live system would do — if anything this biases the OOS number conservative. Before sizing real capital: re-validate the trading-rule grid on an independent third window, and carry the filter state forward instead of restarting it.
